# Study Plan Format

`STUDY_PLAN.md` is the learner-visible, revisable course map. Keep it short enough to scan and update. It records instructional decisions, not every interaction.

## Template

```markdown
# Study Plan: <Topic>

Plan version: <number or date>

## Learner Frame

- Intended capability:
- Real use or deadline:
- Time budget and constraints:
- Relevant prior experience, self-reported:
- Demonstrated starting evidence:
- Language or access needs that affect delivery:
- Unknown prerequisites:

## Units

| Unit | Observable objective | Prerequisites | Main activity | Mastery evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ... | ... | ... | ... | Not assessed |

## Current State

- Demonstrated:
- Partial or fragile:
- Needs practice:
- Prerequisite gaps:
- Unresolved questions:
- Next action:

## Plan Changes

- <date/version>: <Advance, Consolidate, Backfill, or Replan> because <observed evidence or agreed constraint>.
```

## Rules

- Use chapters when a linear chapter structure fits; otherwise use learning units.
- Keep self-reported knowledge separate from demonstrated evidence.
- Use qualitative status: `Not assessed`, `Demonstrated`, `Partial`, `Needs practice`, or `Prerequisite gap`.
- Define mastery evidence before teaching the unit. Match it to the objective: explanation, retrieval, calculation, diagnosis, implementation, critique, artifact, performance, or transfer.
- Update the plan after evidence changes sequencing, practice needs, constraints, or goals. Record only material changes.
- Use `Advance`, `Consolidate`, `Backfill`, or `Replan` for the next instructional decision, and state the evidence.
- When a revised short-term target differs from the original goal, retain both and explain the relationship in `Plan Changes`.
- Do not invent a 0-100 mastery score, probability, or similar numeric estimate without a defined, valid scoring instrument.
- Do not store sensitive personal details or infer age, disability, competence, or learning style from weak signals.
- Completion means the stated capability is demonstrated; it does not imply certification, professional competence, or durable retention.
